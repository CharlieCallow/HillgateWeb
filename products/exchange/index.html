Currency and crypto-asset exchange.

Two distinct services under two distinct licences. Fiat currency exchange runs under Hillgate's electronic money institution licence. Crypto-asset exchange of supported electronic money tokens runs under Hillgate's MiCAR licence. Hillgate is the counterparty to every transaction.

Fiat currency exchange

Provided under the electronic money institution licence granted by De Nederlandsche Bank (R200389).

Currency exchange

What this service is

Hillgate exchanges one supported fiat currency for another. The client transacts with Hillgate as principal: Hillgate is the counterparty to the exchange. The client requests a rate, Hillgate offers a rate, and the client chooses whether to transact at that rate. There is no order book and no third-party broker.

Settled funds move between the client's e-money balances. Settlement speed depends on the currency and rail involved. SEPA-area currencies typically settle same day. Currencies routed via correspondent banking can take longer.

How pricing works

There is no per-transaction fee table for fiat currency exchange. Pricing is embedded in the rate Hillgate offers. The rate quoted to the client is the rate executed: the spread to a reference rate is not unbundled at execution time.

Commercial terms are agreed bilaterally per client and reflect expected monthly volume, currencies in scope, and operational requirements. Bespoke terms are documented in the account agreement, which prevails over any indicative pricing.

Who uses it

  • Corporate treasury functions converting between currencies for invoice settlement.
  • Asset managers consolidating multi-currency positions inside a single institutional account.
  • Fintech partners offering currency conversion to their own counterparties under a documented programme.

How clients access this service

Clients use the web application or the Hillgate API after onboarding. Both channels support request-for-quote, acceptance and execution. The per-channel matrix is on the How it works page.

Crypto-asset exchange

What this service is

Hillgate exchanges supported electronic money tokens (EMTs) for funds, and for other supported EMTs. The client transacts with Hillgate as principal: the client is contractually facing Hillgate, not an underlying venue or wallet counterparty. Hillgate sources liquidity from the EMT issuers, authorised distributors and other professional liquidity providers in line with its execution approach.

At launch, the supported EMTs are EURC (issued by Circle) and USDC (issued by Circle). Additional EMTs will be added through the firm's product approval process. Tokens that are not EMTs are not in scope of this service.

How the price is formed

Pricing is request-for-quote. The client requests a quote through the web application or the API for a specified pair and notional. Hillgate returns a firm, all-in price valid for 15 seconds. If the client accepts within that window, Hillgate executes at the quoted price. If the window expires, the client requests a new quote.

The all-in price has two components, both disclosed on the quote: a base spread that reflects the cost of conversion attributable to the issuer or authorised distributor (minting, redemption, directly attributable settlement costs), and a commercial margin agreed with the client. The base spread is the same for all clients for a given EMT. The commercial margin is agreed bilaterally and reflects monthly and anticipated transaction volume. The full fee structure is on the MiCAR page.

Settlement

Settlement is near-instant. The fiat leg debits or credits the client's e-money balance held under the EMI licence. The crypto-asset leg debits or credits the client's custodied balance held under the MiCAR licence. Both legs are recorded inside the institutional account.

EMT-to-EMT conversions

An exchange from one EMT to another EMT (for example, USDC to EURC) is executed as two independent transactions: a redemption of the first EMT into its reference fiat, followed by a purchase of the second EMT against the reference fiat of the second EMT. The client sees both legs on the trade confirmation and in the account statement. The two-leg model reflects how liquidity is sourced from EMT issuers and authorised distributors and how each EMT settles against its reference currency.

How clients access this service

Clients use the web application or the Hillgate API after onboarding. The request-for-quote, quote acceptance and execution flows are available in both channels. The per-channel matrix is on the How it works page.

Market risk

Prices can move significantly in short periods and are influenced by factors including supply and demand, macroeconomic conditions, technological developments and regulatory action. Custody risk

Crypto-assets held in custody are exposed to risks distinct from those of fiat money held as e-money. While Hillgate maintains institutional-grade custody arrangements with segregated wallets, multi-party computation and offline backup, no custody arrangement is risk-free. Possible failure modes include private-key compromise, technical defects in the custody stack, custodian insolvency and external attack.

Operational risk

Operational risk arises from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events. Examples include failed reconciliations, mis-routed payments, identity-verification failures, errors in sanctions screening and third-party service interruptions. Hillgate maintains controls calibrated to the size and nature of its activities, but residual operational risk remains and is not zero.

Technology risk

Technology risk includes the availability and integrity of the Hillgate platform, third-party infrastructure, blockchain networks and the cryptographic primitives on which crypto-assets depend. The blockchain networks used to settle crypto transactions are not controlled by Hillgate. Forks, congestion, protocol upgrades and consensus failures may delay or prevent execution. Regulatory and tax risk

The regulatory framework for crypto-assets is recent and evolving. Changes to MiCAR, its delegated acts, national implementing measures or supervisory expectations may alter the scope, conditions or cost of the services Hillgate offers. The tax treatment of crypto-assets varies by jurisdiction and by client status; Hillgate does not provide tax advice. Clients should consult their own advisors.

Transfer risk

Transfers of crypto-assets to and from external wallets are subject to travel-rule data requirements, name-matching, and blockchain confirmation times. Transfers sent to an incorrect address or to a wallet outside the supported network may be irrecoverable. SEPA and SWIFT transfers are subject to the rules of the relevant payment scheme, including cut-off times, intermediary fees and recall procedures.

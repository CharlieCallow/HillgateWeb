Applicability

The risks below apply to services currently offered, and to crypto-asset services that will become available following MiCAR authorisation by the AFM. The text identifies which risks are specific to each service group. Counterparties should read this disclosure before any commitment to use the services and should obtain independent advice if uncertain whether a service is appropriate.

Market risk

FX rates between fiat currencies are subject to market risk. Indicative reference rates shown in the application are not guaranteed execution rates. Adverse moves between the quote, acceptance and execution windows may result in a different outcome from the indicative rate. Liquidity in less-traded pairs may be limited outside core hours.

Crypto-assets, where supported in a future state, can be highly volatile. Counterparties may lose some or all of the funds committed to a position. Prices are influenced by supply and demand, regulatory action, technical developments in the underlying networks and macroeconomic conditions.

Custody risk

Crypto-asset custody is not currently offered. Once the MiCAR authorisation is granted, crypto-assets held in custody on behalf of counterparties will be exposed to risks distinct from those of fiat funds held under the EMI safeguarding arrangement. These include private-key compromise, defects in the custody stack, custodian insolvency and external attack. Custody arrangements will be detailed alongside the service when it is launched.

Crypto-assets are not deposits and are not covered by the Dutch deposit guarantee scheme or any equivalent compensation scheme.

Operational risk

Operational risk arises from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events. Examples include failed reconciliations, mis-routed payments, identity-verification failures and third-party service interruptions. Controls are calibrated to the size and nature of the firm's activities. Residual operational risk is not zero.

Technology risk

Technology risk covers the availability and integrity of the web application, the payment API, and the third-party infrastructure on which the firm relies, including payment-scheme connectivity and, in due course, the public blockchain networks used to settle crypto-asset transactions. Counterparties bear residual risk from third-party outages outside the firm's control.

Regulatory and tax risk

The regulatory framework for crypto-assets is recent. Changes to MiCAR, its delegated acts, national implementing measures or supervisory expectations may alter the scope, conditions or cost of services once authorised. The tax treatment of crypto-assets and FX transactions varies by jurisdiction and by counterparty type. Hillgate does not provide tax advice. Counterparties should consult their own advisors.

Transfer risk

SEPA and SWIFT transfers are subject to the rules of the relevant payment scheme, including cut-off times, intermediary fees and recall procedures. Funds sent to an incorrect IBAN may be irrecoverable.

Crypto-asset transfers, once supported, will be subject to travel-rule data requirements under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113, name-matching against beneficiary records, and blockchain confirmation times. Transfers sent to an incorrect address or to a wallet on an unsupported network may be irrecoverable.
